01
Program reporting

Comparing healthcare program cohorts

Participant and nonparticipant comparisons across multiple reporting years.

What I worked on

Prepared financial, utilization, risk, and preventive-care comparisons for a healthcare program refresh. The reporting brief required specific cohort rules, including the exclusion of nonclaimants.

Decisions and checks

Aligned population criteria, claimant exclusions, reporting periods, and measure definitions. Checked that the report outputs and presentation used the same populations and labels.

Deliverable

Updated reports and presentation comparisons. The results described differences between groups; they did not establish that the program caused those differences.

03 — My approach

Before the chart,
the right questions.

A changed enrollment rule or denominator can change the story a report tells. Here’s how I work through those details.

Analyst’s note / 01

What decision will
this report support?

A cost trend, a program comparison, and a quality measure need different definitions. I start with the business question and the reporting period.

From the case notes

A participant comparison describes a difference between groups. It does not, by itself, measure the program’s effect.

Analyst’s note / 02

Who belongs in
the denominator?

I check enrollment, inclusion and exclusion rules, condition criteria, and time periods before comparing populations.

From the claims demonstration

The PMPM denominator includes enrolled months with no claims. Excluding them would change the result.

Analyst’s note / 03

Do the definitions
and totals agree?

I check filters, numerators, denominators, and source-to-output consistency. A missing benchmark and a calculation discrepancy need different investigations.

From the migration case note

Top-N and physician-type filters can change a total. I document that context before treating the difference as an error.

Analyst’s note / 04

What can we say
with confidence?

I keep workbook and presentation labels aligned, document the reporting rules, and make unresolved questions visible.

A useful reporting habit

Put the finding next to its definition and limitation, so the reader can interpret the number in context.
